Your first week of kickboxing: 7 things to know before you glove up

Marcus Reed, Head of Combat Sports (sample)November 12, 20268 min read
Contents

    Just signed up for your first kickboxing class and feeling a little nervous? That’s completely normal. Most beginners walk into their first combat class with two worries: not being able to keep up, and getting hurt. Here are the seven things our coaches tell every new member in week one.

    01Arrive 10 minutes early

    Your first session needs a little setup time: stowing your things, filling in a health form, borrowing gloves and wrapping your hands. Arriving right on time usually means missing the warm-up, and the warm-up is what keeps you injury-free.

    02Eat a light snack 60–90 minutes before

    A full stomach makes rotating your hips and jumping uncomfortable; an empty one can leave you lightheaded. A banana, a cup of yogurt or a slice of toast with peanut butter is plenty. Sip water throughout the day instead of chugging it right before class.

    Snack ideas (for reference)

    • Banana + plain yogurt
    • Whole-grain toast + a boiled egg
    • A small sweet potato + a glass of milk

    If you have a digestive condition or diabetes, check with your doctor or a registered dietitian.

    03Learn to wrap your hands

    Hand wraps keep your wrists and knuckles stable when you punch. Loop three times around the wrist, cross over the back of the hand, weave between the fingers, then lock it off at the wrist. Your coach will wrap them with you on day one; by your third class, you’ll do it yourself.

    04Technique first, power later

    Hitting hard with poor form is the fastest way to sore shoulders and wrists. In week one, focus on three things: your stance, bringing your hands back to guard after every strike, and turning your hips with each punch.

    A good strike is one that comes back to the right place. The power shows up on its own after a few weeks.

    05Listen to your body, not the person next to you

    Everyone starts from a different fitness level. If you feel dizzy, get chest tightness or sharp joint pain, stop and tell your coach. Doing fewer rounds is nothing to worry about; overdoing it is what sidelines people for weeks.

    06Next-day soreness is normal

    Shoulders, calves and abs usually feel it 24–48 hours later. Light walking, stretching and good sleep help you recover. If the pain lingers, swells or sits in one specific spot, get it checked by a doctor.

    07Keep to two sessions a week

    Two sessions a week in your first month is enough for your body to adapt. Add a Yoga or Pilates class to stretch it out if you like. After 8–12 weeks, your coach will let you know when you’re ready for Level 2.

    • Weeks 1–2: 2 Kickboxing Level 1 classes
    • Weeks 3–4: 2 Kickboxing + 1 Yoga
    • Month 2: keep the rhythm, add short conditioning at home
    This article is general information. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, an old injury or are pregnant, check with your doctor before training.
